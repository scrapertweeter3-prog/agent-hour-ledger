Agent hours are priced twice

A coding agent billed at $6 an hour sounds like a steal next to a contractor at $45. Forty booked hours a week is $240, which is $12,480 across a 52 week year. The same calendar books $93,600 for the contractor. On sticker price alone the agent wins by $81,120 and the argument ends before it starts.

The missing line item is the reading. An hour of agent output is worth what it costs to check, and checking takes a person. Assume fifteen minutes of review per agent hour at that same $45 rate: every agent hour quietly carries another $11.25 of human time, which puts the true price at $17.25 an hour and $35,880 a year. The agent still wins, but the real gap is $57,720, and the reviewer is on the payroll either way.

The sticker invites a bad comparison because an hour is an hour for the contractor and a blended price for the agent, one that hides how much output a human must reread before trusting it. Skip the review and the $6 hour looks free until the first wrong migration reaches production, and then the hour costs what the outage costs.

Nobody prices the reading.
